One curriculum in three divisions, from a child’s first lessons about Allah to a six-semester course in the Islamic sciences.

  • Group classes by semester
  • Ages 3 to adult

Overview

Darul Arqam’s Alimiyah and Islamic Studies Program is designed to provide structured Islamic education to learners at different stages of life. Children, teenagers, young adults and mature learners have different educational needs, learning abilities, levels of maturity and life responsibilities. Therefore, the institution offers three separate academic divisions under one unified educational framework.

The curriculum aims to develop sound understanding of Islamic beliefs; knowledge of the Qur’an and its teachings; understanding of Hadith and the Prophetic tradition; practical knowledge of Islamic worship; Arabic language proficiency; awareness of Islamic history and civilization; good character, manners and personal responsibility; and the ability to study and apply Islamic knowledge appropriately.

Placement. Age ranges are general placement guides. Where age ranges overlap, particularly at 12 and 25, placement will be determined by academic readiness, maturity, previous learning and the student’s needs. Age alone will not determine the academic level.

Ages 3–12

Junior Islamic Studies

The Junior Islamic Studies Program introduces children to Islam in a positive, engaging and age-appropriate manner. The objective is to develop love for Allah, the Qur’an, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and Islamic values while establishing essential knowledge and daily religious habits. Teaching may include stories, illustrations, guided conversations, repetition, activities, memorization, simple reading exercises and practical demonstrations.

  • Understand the basic requirements of Wudu and Salah
  • Demonstrate good manners and Islamic conduct
  • Recognize important events from the life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
  • Understand selected Hadith and apply their basic lessons
  • Recognize Arabic letters, words and simple expressions
  • Develop positive attitudes towards learning and worship

Assessment

  • Oral questions and answers
  • Qur’anic reading and memorization
  • Short written exercises and quizzes
  • Practical demonstration of Wudu and Salah
  • Story comprehension
  • Vocabulary and Arabic reading exercises
  • Behavioral and participation observations
  • Term-end assessments

Assessment will consider understanding and development, not only memorization or written examinations.

Students complete each stage after demonstrating the required learning outcomes. Completion of Stage C may prepare a student for a more structured Islamic Studies pathway, subject to placement assessment.

Ages 12–25

Youth and Young Adults Alimiyah

This program is designed for teenagers and young adults seeking systematic Islamic knowledge. It introduces the major Islamic disciplines and gradually develops the ability to read, understand and engage with Islamic texts, combining foundational learning with progressively advanced study in Qur’an, Hadith, Aqidah, Fiqh, Arabic and Islamic intellectual history.

Ages 25+

Adult Alimiyah

The Adult Alimiyah Program is designed for mature learners seeking structured Islamic knowledge while managing professional, family and personal responsibilities. Adult students may have different educational backgrounds, from traditional madrasah study to little formal Islamic education. The pathway therefore combines foundational knowledge, deeper study and practical application with flexible pacing.

Language of instruction

English will be the primary language for international academic communication and instruction where appropriate. Arabic will be taught as a subject and used progressively for reading and understanding Islamic texts. Clarification in another language may be provided when necessary.

Fiqh and scholarly differences

Darul Arqam welcomes students from diverse Muslim backgrounds. Essential practices and jurisprudential principles will be taught clearly and responsibly. At advanced levels, students will be introduced to recognized juristic differences, their evidence and scholarly methods. Legitimate differences should be approached with knowledge, respect and good manners.

Certification

Certificates reflect the program and academic level actually completed.

They are certificates of completion issued by Darul Arqam. They are not an accredited degree or a government-recognized qualification, and are not presented as equivalent to another institution’s Alimiyah or Dars-e-Nizami qualification.
